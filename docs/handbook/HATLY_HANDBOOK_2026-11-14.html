1. Executive summary and current business position

Hatly is a multi-vendor local-commerce and delivery marketplace for Belbeis (Sharkia). Customers order from registered stores, or ask Hatly to buy or carry something from any place ("Hatly Service": هاتلي من مكان / وصّلي لمكان). Hatly runs the operation: it accepts orders on behalf of stores when needed, dispatches its own couriers, collects cash, settles with couriers and stores, and earns commission and service revenue.

1.2 What is working well

  • Strong integrity design. Every status change goes through an explicit state table under a database row lock. Money is integer-only and frozen at the moment it is decided (fees, rates, discounts). Financial events are idempotent and append-only. Wallet and loyalty are ledgers, never edited.
  • Operations tooling is complete for the current scale. This includes the Operations board, Stores now, Drivers today with one-step closing and cash recording, exceptions, failed deliveries, manual-transfer verification, alerts with push and sound, 25 reports with CSV/XLSX/PDF export, and an audit of every admin change.
  • Safe automation. Automatic dispatch never re-assigns or releases on its own. Every timer is idempotent, locked against double-runs, and switchable off.

5. Customer journey

5.1 Account

  • Registration and sign-in by phone + password. Phone OTP verification is controlled by CUSTOMER_OTP_ENABLED, which is false in production (no SMS provider), so phones are not verified.
  • Account deletion is a request, handled by Admin (Customers → Deletion requests).

5.2 Discover and build the basket

  • Home: banners, sectors, curated home sections (best-selling cache), stores, offers.
  • A store is visible and orderable only when its branch is active, open (manual switch) and not inside a scheduled closure, and the customer's address is serviceable.
  • The cart can hold several stores (MULTI_VENDOR_ORDERS_ENABLED=true).
  • Products can be priced per piece or by weight. A weighed item is finalised when the store or courier records the actual weight; it must be recorded before pickup/collection.
  • Stock modes: tracked inventory is decremented on order and restored on cancellation; "open" inventory is not tracked.

5.3 Address and serviceability

  • Two address methods: GPS pin (priced by road distance; needs Maps) or MANUAL (pick a delivery zone; fixed zone fee).
  • In production, Maps is off, so only zone addresses can be priced. Today there is one zone (Abu Naceeb, 15 EGP).
  • Error codes the customer can meet:
    • ADDRESS_ZONE_REQUIRED: the address has no zone.
    • DELIVERY_ZONE_UNAVAILABLE: the selected zone is inactive.
    • DISTANCE_PRICING_UNAVAILABLE: a GPS address while Maps is off.

5.4 Checkout

  1. Quote: the server prices everything (items, offers, delivery fee, service charge, wallet use, ETA range) and returns a signed quote token, valid for CHECKOUT_QUOTE_TTL_SECONDS (code default if unset).
  2. Place order with that token. The server re-validates stock, hours, product validity and payment method, then freezes every amount and the fee breakdown on the order. Order numbers look like HT-100050.
  3. For a manual transfer, the app shows the transfer instructions (destination from MANUAL_TRANSFER_DESTINATION). After transferring, the customer taps "I have transferred".

5.5 After the order

  • Live status, timeline and ETA. The ETA is derived only: store readiness + frozen travel time ± ETA_RANGE_BUFFER_MINUTES (15). If any input is unknown, it says "unavailable" and never fakes a number.
  • In-app notifications for: placed, confirmed, preparing, ready, assigned, on the way, delivered or collected, failed delivery, cancelled, part cancelled, item unavailable. Push is not configured in production.
  • Cancellation by the customer (R3/R40):
    • A store order can be cancelled until the goods leave the store: PENDING, CONFIRMED, PREPARING, READY_FOR_PICKUP, or ASSIGNED_TO_DRIVER (not yet picked up). Hatly bears any preparation cost.
    • A Hatly Service part can be cancelled until the courier records the purchase.
    • The customer can never cancel one store's part only.
  • Unavailable item (PD-016): the store marks the item and the customer chooses to continue without it or cancel the whole order. Silence is never consent: the case waits for Operations, who call the customer and record the decision. There is no substitution.
  • Ratings after completion. Wallet and loyalty screens. Support via WhatsApp (CUSTOMER_SERVICE_WHATSAPP_NUMBER).

6.2 Store part lifecycle

PENDING ──accept──▶ CONFIRMED ──start preparing (optional expected-ready time)──▶ PREPARING ──ready──▶ READY_FOR_PICKUP ──courier picks up──▶ PICKED_UP │ │ │ │ └──────── reject (reason REQUIRED, shown to the customer) ───────────┴─────────────────────────┘──▶ CANCELLED
  • Locked when the order has left the store or ended (PICKED_UP, ON_THE_WAY, DELIVERED, COLLECTED, CANCELLED, FAILED_DELIVERY).
  • The order becomes READY_FOR_PICKUP only when all remaining parts are ready; a cancelled part does not block. Automatic dispatch is tried at that moment.
  • Rejecting one part of a multi-store order recalculates the order on the server: subtotal, discount, delivery fee (additional-store charge drops) and total. A prepaid surplus is refunded (wallet back to wallet; a manual transfer is reconciled).
  • Pickup orders: store staff confirm COLLECTED when the customer picks up (Admin with MANAGE_ORDER_ISSUES as fallback).

6.3 Other store tools

  • Branch open/closed switch and scheduled closures with a reason and an explicit end. The branch reopens by itself at the end; there are no open-ended closures.
  • Preparation time per branch (the source of expectedReadyAt; null means no estimate). 3 of 33 production branches have none.
  • Products: a store's change is a product change request that Admin approves or rejects. Admin's own edits apply directly.
  • Stock and stock history, finance (orders, commission owed, closings), earnings, insights, team (manager), and a notifications centre with a new-order alarm.
  • R2: InstaPay orders are invisible to the store until verified.

7. Driver (courier) journey

  1. Sign in (phone + password; accounts are created and approved by Admin; vehicle type sets capacity).
  2. Go online. Only online + available + APPROVED drivers are dispatch candidates. The app reports its presence on every API call. If the setting is on, "app silent for N minutes" raises a DRIVER_OFFLINE alert while the driver holds an order.
  3. Receive an assignment (automatic or manual). The driver must acknowledge within the window (default 5 min). If not, Operations get one alert; nothing is auto-released (R16).
  4. Pick up at each store ("From — go here first"):
    • Weighed items need their weight recorded.
    • Hatly Service: record the purchase amount.
    • For COD the courier pays the stores from the cash he will collect. For electronic orders he pays from his own money and Hatly reimburses.
  5. On the way → Delivered:
    • Enter the cash received; it can't be less than the amount due.
    • Change the customer leaves becomes a cash remainder claim, credited to the customer's wallet only after Admin approval.
    • The proof photo is optional.
  6. Failed delivery (from PICKED_UP or ON_THE_WAY) with a reason:
    • Reasons: customer unreachable, refused, address not found, unsafe location, vehicle issue, other.
    • Status is terminal; Operations decide the outcome: resolved, no refund or refund customer.
  7. No reject / release button. Problems → "Call Operations" (they reassign).
  8. Earnings:
    • The current (open) shift shows per order: delivery fee, Hatly commission, cash held, goods paid to the store, change.
    • Closed shifts show their reference. All numbers come from the server (one formula).

9.3 Delivery and acknowledgement

A delivery row holds the courier, assigned_at, accept_deadline_at (= assignment + window) and acknowledged_at. Opening the delivery or picking up acknowledges it. A missed deadline produces exactly one ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_OVERDUE alert per assignment.

10. Calculations: fees, offers, totals, loyalty

10.1 Store-order delivery fee

Distance method (GPS address, Maps on):
  route  = road distance through the stores (farthest-from-customer first) to the customer, TOTAL route
  rounded = round DOWN to 100 m if the remainder ≤ 20 m, else UP to the next 100 m
  distanceFee = (rounded / 100) × REGISTERED_VENDOR_PRICE_PER_100M_MINOR        (prod: 1.00 EGP per 100 m)
  baseFee     = max(REGISTERED_VENDOR_MINIMUM_DELIVERY_FEE_MINOR, distanceFee)   (prod minimum: 15.00 EGP)
Zone method (MANUAL address — the only one usable in production):
  baseFee = selected zone's fixed fee                                            (prod: Abu Naceeb 15.00 EGP)
Both:
  deliveryFee = baseFee + REGISTERED_VENDOR_ADDITIONAL_VENDOR_FEE_MINOR × (stores − 1)   (prod: +5.00 EGP per extra store)

Every input is frozen in deliveryFeeBreakdownSnapshot, so later setting changes never alter a past order. When a store part is rejected, the fee is re-derived: frozen base + additional × (remaining stores − 1). Example: two stores in the zone = 15 + 5 = 20 EGP; one store rejects, so the fee becomes 15 EGP.

10.2 Hatly Service fee

base fee = zone fee (MANUAL address)  or  road-distance price of ORIGIN → DESTINATION (GPS; no fallback)
charge   = round(base fee × HATLY_SERVICE_DELIVERY_FEE_MULTIPLIER)            (prod 1.5 → 15 EGP zone ⇒ 22.50 EGP)
courier compensation = base fee;  Hatly service revenue = charge − base fee

Route ends come from the customer's choice or the configured defaults (HATLY_SERVICE_DEFAULT_PICKUP_LOCATION, HATLY_SERVICE_DEFAULT_DESTINATION_LOCATION, WASALLY_SERVICE_DEFAULT_DESTINATION_LOCATION). All three are unset in production. The goods' price for BUY_FROM_PLACE is recorded later (courier purchase amount, confirmed by Operations with MANAGE_HATLY_SERVICE_PRICING). Until then the order shows a pending price.

10.3 Offers

  • FLASH_SALE: replaces the product's base price (paid options are added on top). It can be limited to N units per order (the rest at normal price) and has a global allocation, reversed on cancellation.
  • SUBTOTAL_DISCOUNT (incl. coupons): percentage or fixed, on the eligible subtotal of a store part. It can have a minimum eligible subtotal and a maximum discount, and applies once per part.
  • A product can never be in an active flash sale and an active subtotal discount at the same time.
  • Offers are Admin-only and Hatly-funded (they never reduce the store's commission basis). Changing an offer never rewrites existing orders.

10.4 Order totals

subtotal = Σ active store parts' subtotals            (item unit price incl. options × qty, or weighed amount)
discount = Σ parts' offer discounts (segment + per-item flash sale)
total    = subtotal + deliveryFee − discount
amountDue (collected at the door) = total − wallet part (if the wallet paid part)

10.5 Loyalty

Earning (at DELIVERED / COLLECTED, once per order):
  eligible = Σ items (unit × qty − item flash-sale discount) − part subtotal discounts     (fees never count)
  points   = floor( floor(eligible / 100 EGP) × LOYALTY_POINTS_PER_EGP )               (prod 1 point per EGP)
Conversion to wallet (customer request, idempotent):
  minimum = 5 × LOYALTY_WALLET_CONVERSION_POINTS_PER_EGP points, exact multiple only     (prod 30 pts = 1 EGP ⇒ min 150 pts = 5 EGP)

Production effect: 1 point per EGP spent, and 30 points buy 1 EGP. The loyalty value is therefore about 3.3% cash back. The code default (100 pts/EGP) would be 1%. This is a business choice; confirm it is intended.

11. Commission, settlement and closing math

11.1 Commission

  • Store: round(part subtotal × branch.commission_rate_basis_points / 10 000), recognised when the goods leave the store. It is skipped (never assumed) if the branch has no rate. Production: 33/33 branches have a rate.
  • Courier: round(compensation × driver.commission_rate_basis_points / 10 000), recognised at DELIVERED. It is skipped if the courier has no rate. Production: 0/2 couriers have a rate.
  • Rate changes never touch recorded events (each event snapshots its rate). Setting rates needs MANAGE_VENDOR_COMMISSION / MANAGE_DRIVER_COMMISSION.

11.2 Courier closing: one formula, used everywhere

COD order:     goods pass-through = min(goods paid to stores, cash collected)       → excluded from the net
               goods reimbursement = goods the cash did not cover (wallet-paid part)
               cash held = cash collected − pass-through                              (normally the delivery fee)
Electronic:    goods reimbursement = goods paid to stores; no cash
Both:          compensation = delivery fee (compensation basis) − Hatly commission
               NET = compensation + goods reimbursement − cash held − change kept
               NET > 0 → Hatly pays the courier;  NET < 0 → the courier pays Hatly
ExampleGoodsFeeCommissionCashResult
COD3004010340pass-through 300, cash held 40, compensation 30 → courier owes 10
InstaPay30040100reimbursement 300, compensation 30 → Hatly owes 330

Orders whose courier had no rate at delivery are held back ("rate undefined", R43). They are listed, never priced, and must be acknowledged before closing. The closing uses a preview token, so the confirmed figures are exactly what was shown. A closing gets a reference CS-YYYYMMDD-XXXXXX (store: VS-…) and is immutable. Payments against a closing are separate records (method, reference, date, idempotency key). An overpayment needs a second approver.

11.3 Store closing

Per branch and period: gross sales, commission owed (store owes Hatly, since the courier pays the store the full goods price at pickup), parts count, rate-undefined count. Frequency comes from per-vendor terms, else VENDOR_SETTLEMENT_FREQUENCY (empty in production).

13. Dispatch logic

  1. Trigger: an order becomes READY_FOR_PICKUP (all parts ready) and is a delivery order. If AUTOMATIC_DISPATCH_ENABLED is false, an AUTO_ASSIGN_DISABLED alert is raised and the order waits for manual assignment.
  2. First pickup point:
    • The first store in the frozen route (farthest from the customer).
    • Hatly Service BUY: the purchase place.
    • DELIVER: the customer's address. A zone-only address has no point, so it goes to manual dispatch.
  3. Eligibility:
    • Online, available (active orders < capacity), APPROVED, inside an active service area of the first pickup point.
    • Bicycles are excluded beyond MAX_BICYCLE_PICKUP_DISTANCE_METERS (500 m). Without Maps they are always excluded from auto-ranking.
  4. Ranking (R14):
    • Nearest first. Straight-line distance while Maps is off; an ordering heuristic only, never a price.
    • Drivers without a known location come after, in round-robin (least recently assigned first).
  5. Capacity (R18): admin override, else the vehicle type's max active deliveries, else 1. Availability is recomputed at every assign, release, delivery and cancellation.
  6. Assignment sets the acknowledgement deadline (window 1–60 min, default 5). No automatic release or re-assignment ever (R13/R16/R17). Switching dispatch mode never touches existing assignments.
  7. No eligible driver: the order stays READY_FOR_PICKUP with a NO_ELIGIBLE_DRIVER alert. A background retry runs at most once per alert per interval. DRIVER_NOT_RESPONDING is never retried automatically.
  8. Manual: Admin with ASSIGN_REASSIGN_ORDERS picks from the ranked list (the same ranking). Assigning outside the ranking needs OVERRIDE_DISPATCH. The backend refuses with a reason: AT_CAPACITY, OFFLINE, payment gate, pickup order.

19. Security

19.1 What is in place

  • Dashboard sessions:
    • HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict, Secure cookies; the access and refresh tokens are never readable by page scripts.
    • CSRF token is an HMAC of the session id; Origin is checked.
    • Refresh and logout require a custom header.
  • Mobile apps: bearer JWT (access 15 min) + refresh tokens stored server-side in Redis.
  • Brute force:
    • Per-IP route throttles, from 3 to 300 requests per minute by route.
    • Per-account failed-login limiter; it counts unknown phones too, so it reveals nothing.
    • Wrong MFA codes count against the account.
  • MFA for staff:
    • TOTP, secret encrypted with AES-256-GCM, replay-protected, single-use recovery codes, all audited.
    • Break-glass reset by a super admin.
    • Enforcement policy DASHBOARD_ADMIN_MFA_REQUIRED.
  • Authorization: role + permission guard on every admin route. A public-route allowlist and a hostile-id sweep are enforced by tests. Ownership checks for store staff, couriers and customers are re-checked under row locks.
  • Data protection: customer PII is masked without VIEW_CUSTOMER_PII; R2 hides unpaid transfers from stores; exports are permission-gated; the audit log is append-only.
  • Money integrity: integer minor units, frozen snapshots, idempotency keys, append-only ledgers, maker-checker approvals.
  • Operations: env-check at boot (refuses unsafe production config), release bundles scanned for secrets, backups + restore drill before every migration.

19.2 Findings (production)

  • MFA not enrolled for the only super admin, and the policy is off. Enrol now, then set DASHBOARD_ADMIN_MFA_REQUIRED=true. The encryption key must be set first; env-check enforces it.
  • Single privileged account: create named ADMIN accounts per person from templates; keep the super admin for emergencies.
  • Customer phones unverified (OTP off): fake numbers can place COD orders. Mitigate with first-order confirmation calls until SMS is live.
  • Backups are not off-site (only on the same host and the owner's Mac).
  • Known residuals from the security milestone: private media are capability URLs (signed per-read access planned); driver history keeps past customers' phones (redaction planned).
